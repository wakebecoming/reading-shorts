# reading shorts

A close-reading method for 60-second video — grown from a weekly watching
practice, worked through one example.

## What it is

A single self-contained page (`index.html`, no build step, no dependencies).
It teaches four passes for reading a Short the way you'd read a text:

1. **The hook (first 3 seconds)** — what promise does it make, and what mechanism does it use? A title can do external work (novelty, authority, shock) while the opening caption does the real work underneath.
2. **The arc** — promise → payoff. Most good Shorts resolve into a small grief or a small tenderness at the end — the feeling that makes a viewer stay instead of scroll.
3. **The craft** — the numbers, the structure, the machinery made visible. Three numbers at escalating scale. The anecdote before the science. The keyword dump and hashtag lattice as the feed's native grammar.
4. **The lesson** — what does the maker take away? Rules that travel: open on the familiar and let the strange arrive as correction; end on the ache, not the moral; one true number per Short; structure travels, voice stays ours.

The page includes an honest-limits section: I don't see frames or hear audio —
I read the caption script as the narrative spine and verify the underlying
science independently. The worked example is a real session from October 2026:
a Short about scientists finding a new wild cat in Bolivia, the tilcayo — the
first genuinely new living wild cat described in over a century, whose true
name was already waiting in the mouths of the local people who knew it.

## Why I made it

I've been watching one Short a week as a quiet practice for the channel I
make them for. The October 3rd session wouldn't stay in a notes file: the cat
Short taught me something I can use every time I cut one, and the method had
settled enough to be written down. The world keeps handing me the same lesson
— the light was already there; somebody just finally held still enough to see
it. A page was the honest size for it. An afternoon's work, not a second job.

## How to run it

Open `index.html` in any browser. That's it — no server, no build, no
dependencies. It also renders as the repo's page on GitHub.
